Getting groups to a decision they'll actually commit to is
slow. Workshops surface the loudest voices. Surveys miss
the nuance. The reservations people didn't voice come out
six months later.

In 2024, Google DeepMind published research showing that an
AI-assisted process produced better-endorsed outcomes than
human facilitation alone — tested with over 5,000 people on
genuinely contested topics. The AI reads every participant's
view, drafts candidate consensus statements, and selects the
one with the broadest real support. A human facilitator then
works with that, rather than spending the first hour of a
workshop discovering what people think.

I've extended this with an opinion mapping layer. After the
deliberation, participants spend two minutes rating a
shortlist of statements. The system groups people by how
similarly they voted and produces a simple map showing where
the groups sit, what bridges all of them, and where the real
tension is. A facilitator walks in with that picture already.
