Getting groups to a decision they'll actually commit to is
slow. Workshops surface the loudest voices. Surveys miss
the nuance. The reservations people didn't voice come out
six months later.

This tool tries to fix that by running two things in
parallel from a single set of participant opinions.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  TRACK A: QUICK AI READ
----------------------------------------------------------------

As soon as opinions are in, an AI reads all responses,
generates candidate consensus statements, and selects the
one with the broadest predicted support (Schulze voting).
Turnaround is minutes.

This is useful for advance planning — narrowing real-world
options, briefing a facilitator, getting a working
hypothesis before the democratic results are back.

Based on Google DeepMind's Habermas Machine research
(2024, tested with 5,000+ people on contested political
topics).


----------------------------------------------------------------
  TRACK B: DEMOCRATIC VOTE
----------------------------------------------------------------

The same opinions are broken into short atomic statements —
one idea each — and sent out as a voting form. Participants
vote Agree / Pass / Disagree. Takes about a week to collect.

The votes reveal not just which statements are popular but
whether there are distinct opinion groups and which
statements genuinely bridge all of them. This is the
democratic anchor for the final discussion — grounded in
what people actually chose, not what the AI predicted.

Based on Polis (Computational Democracy Project), used by
governments and organisations for large-scale deliberation.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  THE WORKSHOP
----------------------------------------------------------------

Both outputs feed a short facilitated workshop where the
group finalises the decision. The AI has done the
groundwork — surfacing what people think, where opinion
clusters, and where the real tension is. The workshop
focuses on the actual decision.

Typical participant time: about 10 minutes across all steps,
spread over a week, plus the workshop.
